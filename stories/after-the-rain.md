# After the Rain

The rain had stopped an hour ago but the city still smelled like it.

Wet concrete. Ozone. The metallic tang of the subway rising through the grates. She stood under the awning of a closed bookstore, coat open, cigarette forgotten between her fingers. The streetlights made everything look like it was underwater.

I should have kept walking.

I didn’t.

“You’re late,” she said.

“I didn’t know we had plans.”

“We always have plans.” She dropped the cigarette and ground it out with the toe of her boot. “You just pretend you don’t remember.”

We had done this before. Three times. Maybe four. Always after something — a gallery opening, a mutual friend’s disastrous party, a night that went too long and too strange. Always the same careful distance that collapsed the moment one of us touched the other.

Tonight the distance was already gone.

She stepped in close enough that I could feel the rain still clinging to her coat. Her hand found the front of my shirt, not quite pulling, just resting there like a claim.

“My place is closer,” she said.

“I know.”
